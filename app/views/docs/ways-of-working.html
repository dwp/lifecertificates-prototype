Design documentation

{{ pageName }}

Agreed approaches to research, evidence, documentation, collaboration and prototype management.

Collaboration happens throughout all stages of work. Designers, researchers, business analysts, product managers and subject matter experts contribute to planning, research, design, review and documentation activities.

Research

Research should inform design decisions, challenge assumptions and guide future iterations of the prototype. Research activities should focus on understanding users, their needs, the current service and areas of uncertainty.

Evidence

Research findings should be synthesised into evidence that supports decision-making. Evidence should help teams understand user needs, assess confidence in findings, identify impacts on users and prioritise improvements.

Prototype

The prototype should represent the team's current understanding of the service. Design decisions should be informed by research and evidence, with assumptions tested and refined through iterative design and evaluation.

Iteration

The prototype should evolve continuously in response to new evidence, feedback and learning. Each iteration should improve understanding of the service and inform future research activities.

Documentation

Design and Research Knowledge

A shared, non-versioned source of evolving knowledge maintained by designers and researchers.

It should be updated continuously as work progresses and inform current and future decision-making.

Includes

  • Research and evidence
  • Design decisions and rationale
  • Design debt
  • Links to supporting artefacts and findings

Research and evidence

Significant findings should be recorded, including confidence, user impact, recommendations and outcomes.

Design decisions

Key design decisions should be recorded alongside the rationale and supporting evidence that informed them.

Design debt

Known gaps, limitations, unanswered questions, assumptions and areas requiring further validation should be recorded and reviewed regularly.

Prototype Version Overview

Each meaningful version of the prototype should have an associated Version Overview.

Create a new version when changes affect

  • Service behaviour
  • Service meaning or interpretation
  • Research outcomes
  • Measures of success
  • Users, roles or responsibilities
  • The understanding of known gaps

Includes

  • Service or prototype overview
  • Users and roles
  • Known gaps
  • Measures of success
  • Decisions specific to that version
  • Change history

The Version Record should make it possible for anyone reviewing the prototype to understand what changed, why it changed and the current state of the service.

Together, the Knowledge Record and Version Record ensure that the prototype remains evidence-led, traceable and easy to understand as the service evolves.
